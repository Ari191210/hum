# Outreach kit (for our researcher)

Goal: 2–4 real conversations with people who work with non-speaking people with motor impairments. Their honest feedback (including "this won't work because…") is worth more to judges than any number we invent.

## Who to contact

- Occupational therapists (OTs) and speech-language therapists (SLPs / speech therapists)
- Special educators and special schools
- Disability organisations, especially ones working with cerebral palsy, ALS/MND, stroke or AAC
- Rehabilitation departments of hospitals or colleges

How to find them without guessing: search "AAC India", "speech therapist [your city]", "cerebral palsy school [your city]", "occupational therapy department [nearby hospital]". Teachers, family friends and school counsellors often know someone. LinkedIn works well for therapists. Keep a simple list: name, role, organisation, how you contacted them, date, reply.

Send to 10–15 people. Expect maybe 2–4 replies in 48 hours; that is normal.

## Message (WhatsApp / email / LinkedIn)

**Subject:** 15 minutes of feedback on a student assistive-tech prototype?

> Hello [Name],
>
> I'm [your name], a student at [school] in [city]. My team of three is building a prototype in a 48-hour app competition, and we would be very grateful for your expert opinion.
>
> The idea: a free Android app that lets someone who cannot speak, and cannot reliably use a touchscreen, "press a switch" just by moving their hand near the phone. The phone plays an inaudible tone and listens to the echo off the hand (like sonar). That movement selects phrases on a simple scanning communication board ("Water", "Help", "Pain"...), which the phone speaks aloud. Nothing is filmed or uploaded; everything stays on the phone.
>
> This is a student prototype, not a medical device, and we don't want to make claims we can't back up. We'd love to hear who this could help, who it couldn't, and what would make it unusable in real life.
>
> Could you spare 15 minutes for a call in the next day or two, or reply with written thoughts? We can also send a 30-second video of it working.
>
> We will only quote you or use your name if you give us permission, and you can say no to that and still help.
>
> Thank you so much,
> [Name], [phone/email]

**Short follow-up (send once, after ~12 hours if no reply):**

> Hi [Name], just a gentle follow-up on my message about our student assistive-tech prototype. Even a two-line reply with your honest opinion would help us a lot. Thank you!

## Interview guide (6 questions, ~15 minutes)

Start of call, read aloud:
> "Thank you for your time. This is a student prototype, not a medical device. With your permission I'll take notes. At the end I'll ask whether we may quote you, and you can say no."

1. **Who would benefit?** From your experience, which people — conditions, ages, situations — might find a "move your hand near the phone to select" switch useful?
2. **Who wouldn't?** Who would this clearly *not* work for, and why? (e.g. no voluntary movement, severe tremor, visual impairment)
3. **What would make it unusable?** In a real home or classroom, what would make someone give up on it within a week? (false triggers, setup, noise, the phone falling, fatigue…)
4. **Setup and caregivers:** Who would set this up and position the phone? What worries would a caregiver have about using it all day?
5. **Cost and access:** What do the people you work with currently use to communicate or as switches, and what gets in the way of getting them (cost, availability, training)? *(Note any prices they mention as "according to [person]" — we will not publish prices we can't source.)*
6. **Permission to quote:** May we quote what you said in our project write-up and video? If yes, how should we name you (full name and role / role only / anonymous)?

Bonus if time allows: "If you could change one thing about the idea, what would it be?"

## Rules we never break

- No quote, name or photo without explicit permission (written in chat/email is best — screenshot it).
- Never film anyone without consent. If a teammate demonstrates the app, the video must not suggest they have a disability.
- Don't promise anything (availability, price, medical benefit).
